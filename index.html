01

Introduction to the PPW Module

Module overview, assessments & expectations

Description

Our lecturer walked us through the module structure, assessment breakdown and expectations. We learned PPW has no final exam. We have a quiz (20%), portfolio (50%) and mock interview (30%).

Feelings

I honestly didn't expect much at first. But hearing about the portfolio and live interview made it feel very personal and real, bringinga mix of nerves and quiet excitement.

Evaluation

The session was well-organized and gave me a clear picture of what lay ahead. The only thing that unsettled me was realising I had never built a professional portfolio before.

Analysis

My nervousness came from having to present myself, including my goals, skills and personality. That felt quite exposing. However, I realized that this is exactly what the professional world requires.

Conclusion

PPW is not just another module. It's designed to build my professional identity from the ground up. That reframe changed everything.

Action Plan

I decided to take every session seriously and start collecting portfolio materials early rather than scrambling at the deadline.

02

Presentation Skills

Structure · Signposting · Body language · Slide design

Description

We covered the three-part structure (beginning, middle, end), signposting phrases, slide design rules and the five body language elements (eyes, voice, expression, appearance and posture).

Feelings

Presentations have always made me anxious. This session felt relevant and slightly nerve-wracking, but I was relieved the techniques were practical and immediately applicable.

Evaluation

Learning signposting phrases like 'Moving on, I'd like to look at…' was my top takeaway. I also realised I'd been making my slides far too text-heavy.

Analysis

My past struggles came from ignoring delivery and focusing only on content. A clear structure and rehearsed signposting makes a huge difference to how an audience experiences a talk.

Conclusion

A strong conclusion matters as much as a good opening. Because the audience remembers the last thing you say. That was a genuinely new insight for me.

Action Plan

I'll limit each slide to 3-5 points, rehearse aloud at least three times and record myself once to review my own body language before any future presentation.

03

Business Writing & Formal Letter Writing

Clarity · Conciseness · Formal letter formats

Description

We explored the principles of professional business writing and learned to write formal letters including complaints, invitations, apologies and job applications, all in correct format.

Feelings

Switching from casual messaging to formal writing felt like a gear change. A few in-class activity answers came out too informal, but a very useful wake-up call.

Evaluation

The rewriting exercises were the most effective part. Seeing 'obtain' instead of 'get' in side-by-side comparison made the difference in tone instantly obvious.

Analysis

I had been underestimating how much written communication shapes first impressions at work. Professional writing is a conscious skill that doesn't happen automatically.

Conclusion

Every sentence in a business document must serve the reader. Polite, concise language builds trust far better than vague or informal writing ever could.

Action Plan

Before every professional email or letter, I will make sure it is formal, concise and properly proofread. I will also practise writing one formal letter each week based on different scenarios.

04

Email Writing & Memo Writing

Netiquette · Email structure · Memo format

Description

This session built on last week's writing skills, focusing on professional email structure and memo writing using the TO/FROM/DATE/SUBJECT format.

Feelings

Email felt immediately relevant since I already email lecturers. But I quickly realised I'd been skipping subject lines and using informal greetings. Memo writing was completely new territory.

Evaluation

Ready-made email phrases like 'I am writing with reference to…' gave me a toolkit I can use right away. The memo format was also very clear. However, I wish we had spent more time learning how to politely follow up on unanswered emails.

Analysis

Professional email isn't optional. It's expected. One poorly written email can damage your reputation as much as a poor letter. I hadn't been treating emails seriously enough.

Conclusion

Netiquette - replying promptly, using clear subject lines, maintaining a professional tone, is just as important as the writing itself.

Action Plan

Proper greetings and subject lines in every email from now on. I'll also set up a professional email signature with my name, programme and contact details.

05

Telephone Etiquette

Introduction · Purpose · Conclusion · Tone of voice

Description

We covered the three elements of a business call, plus best practices for tone, clarity, active listening and handling wrong numbers, transfers and message-taking.

Feelings

I was genuinely surprised that phone skills need formal training. Reflecting honestly on my own professional calls, I realised my tone and pacing needed real work.

Evaluation

The insight that a monotone voice signals disinterest to the caller was the most impactful moment. The tips on scheduling and planning calls were also very practical.

Analysis

Phone calls are harder than face-to-face because body language is invisible. That puts far more weight on tone, word choice and pace which is something I had never consciously considered.

Conclusion

A professional phone call deserves the same care as any written document. Every caller should leave the interaction feeling respected, heard and helped.

Action Plan

I'll practise mock professional calls with a friend and script a confident opening phrase, so I never fumble at the start of a business call again.

06

Report Writing

Information · Research · Proposal reports · Groan Factor

Description

We studied the purpose, types and structure of professional reports covering title page, executive summary, intro, body, conclusions and bibliography. The 'GROAN FACTOR' concept stood out.

Feelings

Report writing felt rigid at first, but the session reframed it as a craft. The concept of the executive summary being written last but placed first was genuinely clever and something I had never considered before.

Evaluation

'It's all about the reader' was the one phrase changed how I think about writing. I recognised several errors I'd made in past reports, including no clear aim, no summary, too much irrelevant information.

Analysis

Poor report writing happens when writers focus on what they know rather than what the reader needs. Editing and cutting are just as important as the initial writing.

Conclusion

Always ask - 'Who is reading this, and what do they actually need?' The executive summary is the most critical page of any long report.

Action Plan

Define purpose and audience before writing. Draft the executive summary last. Ask a peer to review structure, not just grammar, before every future report.

07

How to Face an Interview

Interview types · STAR method · Non-verbal communication

Description

We covered interview types, including in-person, panel, behavioural, technical, case study, virtual, along with what employers assess, non-verbal communication techniques and strategies for answering questions. We ended with a mock interview.

Feelings

Interviews genuinely terrify me. But learning that preparation and body language are entirely within my control made the whole thing feel far more manageable.

Evaluation

The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) gave me a real framework for behavioural questions. The most valuable part of the entire session.

Analysis

My fear came from uncertainty. Now I understand employers are assessing fit, communication and problem-solving, rather thantrying to catch me out. That shift in perspective changes everything.

Conclusion

Interview success is preparation, not luck. Answers, body language, appearance and even the questions you ask. All of it can be planned in advance.

Action Plan

I'll prepare STAR-format answers for common questions, practise in front of a mirror and conduct at least two full mock interviews before the formal assessment.

Overall Reflection

Seven sessions in, PPW changed how I see myself as a future professional. I used to think communication skills were secondary to technical knowledge. Now I understand they are what allow technical knowledge to be seen at all. The biggest shift has been becoming more intentional. I think before I write, plan before I speak and always consider my audience.

In the professional world, how you communicate matters just as much as what you know.
